# Ontology in life sciences

- **1960:** MeSH illustrates the long history of controlled biomedical terminology.
- **1998/2000:** the Gene Ontology project was built to support shared biological descriptions across model-organism databases; its original publication appeared in 2000.
- **2004:** ChEBI provides a chemical-entity vocabulary and shows why chemical structure, role and evidence need careful modeling.
- **2007:** the OBO Foundry paper describes coordinated evolution of interoperable biomedical ontologies.
- **2008 onward:** HPO supports structured phenotype descriptions for genetic disorders and research.
- **2023:** PrimeKG was published as a multimodal precision-medicine knowledge graph with multiple node and edge types.

The consulting lesson is to keep the ontology, annotations, study records and evidence sources distinct. Missing an annotation is not evidence that a gene product lacks a function. A teaching pack must preserve term versions, evidence codes, references and licenses. Do not use these examples for diagnosis or treatment.

Sources: [Gene Ontology documentation](https://www.geneontology.org/docs/ontology-documentation/), [GO annotation guidance](https://www.geneontology.org/docs/go-annotations/), [OBO Foundry](https://www.nature.com/articles/nbt1346), [HPO](https://obophenotype.github.io/human-phenotype-ontology/), [PrimeKG](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-023-01960-3).
