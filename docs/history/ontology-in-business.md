# Ontology in business

- **1993:** Tom Gruber's work made “formal specification of a conceptualization” a widely cited description of computational ontology.
- **2008 context:** the FIBO community describes financial-governance concerns arising during the financial crisis and the need for shared vocabulary. This is context, not a claim that one event created FIBO.
- **2011:** Schema.org launched as a shared web vocabulary. It demonstrates broad vocabulary coordination, not necessarily OWL reasoning.
- **2013 onward:** PROV-O provides a standard vocabulary for provenance, useful for tracing business assertions and transformations.
- **2020:** FINOS documents that Legend was originally developed by Goldman Sachs and contributed to FINOS in October 2020. Legend is a shared data-modeling platform; this is not a claim that it is an OWL ontology.

In practice, an ontology helps teams agree what a customer, asset, owner or dependency means. It does not decide legal ownership, financial value or operational accountability by itself.

Sources: [Gruber](https://tomgruber.org/writing/ontolingua-kaj-1993/), [FIBO community](https://spec.edmcouncil.org/fibo/page/community), [Schema.org launch](https://search.googleblog.com/2011/06/introducing-schemaorg-search-engines.html), [PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/2013/REC-prov-o-20130430/), [FINOS Legend](https://legend.finos.org/docs/community/case-studies).
